\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
AlphaFold2 inference on an eight-chip TPU v5e slice used one chip unless the program mapped the work to the others.
Among the four ways of mapping the work that we compared, only multi-query \texttt{jax.pmap} ran independent queries on all eight chips.
On the matched grid, eight chips gave 6.53 to about 7.9 times the throughput of one chip. % src: recomputed: V2.speedup_8over1_min, V2.speedup_8over1_max
We batched up to eight queries of 118 residues on one chip with \texttt{jax.vmap}, and the throughput did not rise. % src: results/sweep/batching_sweep.json -> [*].batch_size; recomputed: INPUT.toy_length
Automatic partitioning without sharding annotations did not shorten the call for one query.
For a user with many independent queries, multi-query \texttt{jax.pmap} is a simple way to put every chip of a rented slice to work.
Our measurements used random weights, and most settings ran once on one slice. Future work should repeat the measurements with trained weights.
It should also compare multi-query \texttt{pmap} with eight separate processes running at the same time.
